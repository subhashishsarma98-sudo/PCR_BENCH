Total tubes = (samples × replicates) + positive controls + NTCs. Extra volume is optional and defaults to 0%. Enter the percentage you want to add to the shared PCR mixture.

Why use extra volume? A small amount of mixture can be lost during pipetting and transfer, for example by remaining inside pipette tips or on tube walls. Preparing extra common mixture can help ensure that there is enough to dispense into every planned tube. This is a practical allowance, not an additional PCR tube.
Calculation logic: the calculator first counts all sample and control tubes, then applies the selected extra percentage to the common mixture.
Common-mix equivalents = total tubes × (1 + extra volume / 100)
Example for 10 planned tubes: 0% extra = 10 reaction-equivalents; 5% extra = 10.5 reaction-equivalents; 10% extra = 11 reaction-equivalents.
The extra percentage is applied to the shared reagents, including water. Template DNA is excluded from the common mix and added separately to each tube.
